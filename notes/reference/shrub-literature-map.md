# Shrub and young-tree literature map

Source bundle: local `brushline-shrub-lit-package.zip`, searched 2026-11-13. The bundle is not committed.
It contains 19 items, 16 with open full text, 2 abstract-only records, 1 unavailable record, and 14 PDFs.
Open access does not establish redistribution rights, so the PDFs remain local review copies. This map
keeps citations and the decision-relevant findings only.

## What the bundle changes

It does not provide a validated BC or Pacific Northwest shrub-height trajectory, multi-cycle treatment
response data, or operational forecast-error evidence. It therefore does not replace the P4 prototype
model card or its synthetic validation. It does give a stronger operational baseline and a clear list of
follow-up measurements.

## Decision-relevant evidence

- **Operational cycles.** BCP22 supports the P6 baseline range: transmission about 4–12 years and
  distribution about 3–7 years, with shorter transmission cycles possible. This is a BAU scenario input,
  not a calibrated growth parameter.
- **BC and boreal treatment evidence.** BRU92, YUK14, and ISB16 support the existence of treatment
  effects and the importance of season, method, and resprouting. ISB16 is an early one-year response and
  does not establish a multi-cycle trajectory.
- **Model comparators.** MED23, FVS–FFE, LANDIS-II, VDDT/ST-Sim, FireBGCv2, PHYGROW, ORCHIDEE, and TASS
  remain comparators. None supplies a portable, validated BC corridor shrub height/cover transition.
- **Measurement.** BAH25 and CHE22 support LiDAR-based monitoring and clearance geometry, but not a
  growth or treatment-response calibration.

## Evidence gaps

1. Repeated measurements of shrub and young-tree height, cover, and stem density by species,
   biogeoclimatic zone, and time since treatment in BC or the Pacific Northwest.
2. Matched treatment and control observations over multiple cycles, including method, season, intensity,
   resprouting, and selectivity.
3. Repeat LiDAR and field checks on held-out utility segments, reporting alert lead time, missed
   clearance crossings, and uncertainty.
4. Mode-specific evidence for railway, pipeline, and roadside corridors. Utility evidence does not
   transfer without testing.

## Boundary

This map is a prioritized public-source evidence scan. It is not a systematic review, a field
calibration, or a licence to redistribute the PDFs. The P4 model remains a prototype; P6 uses the
operational cycle ranges and caveats above, not a claim of local biological validation.
