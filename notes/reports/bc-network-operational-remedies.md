# Weather/outage remedies

## Question

Which operational remedy is most effective under the assumed weather/outage calendar: additional
crew/machine capacity, a smaller first-year scope, or a longer window?

The comparison uses all 20 rolling episodes, 50 calendars per episode, 75-day base window, five
fixed outage days, and independent 20% daily weather loss.

## Results

| Remedy | Scenario full completion | Added cost P50 | Total cost P50 | Budget feasible |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Baseline | 13.2% | 0 | 59,875 | 100% |
| +1 shared crew | 13.2% | 10,800 | 70,761 | 0% |
| +1 shared crew + machine | 92.9% | 80,850 | 140,800 | 0% |
| 10% scope reduction | 23.4% | 0 | 53,888 | 100% |
| 90-day window | 41.5% | 0 | 59,875 | 100% |

Capacity cost assumptions: 1,200 CAD per crew-day and 900 CAD per machine-day against a
60,000 CAD first-year budget.

## Interpretation

- Additional crew capacity alone has no effect because mowing and mechanical work also requires a
  machine. A shared crew plus machine is the strongest completion remedy.
- The shared crew plus machine is not budget-feasible at the prototype rates: median total first-year
  cost is about 140,800 CAD against the 60,000 CAD budget.
- Under the fixed budget, the 90-day window is the best budget-feasible completion case at 41.5%;
  the 10% scope reduction reaches 23.4%.
- Capacity is a budget-expansion decision, not a free scheduling fix.

## Limits

- Added capacity and scope reduction are hypothetical tests, not contractor commitments.
- Crew-day and machine-day rates are prototype assumptions, not contractor quotes.
- Scope reduction applies proportionally to work-item hours; it is not a risk-ranked deferral policy.
- Weather and outage assumptions are prototype values, not forecasts or asset-owner calendars.
- Only the first-year shift schedule is tested.
