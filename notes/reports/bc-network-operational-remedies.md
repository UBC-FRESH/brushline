# Weather/outage remedies

## Question

Which operational remedy is most effective under the assumed weather/outage calendar: additional
crew/machine capacity, a smaller first-year scope, or a longer window?

The comparison uses all 20 rolling episodes, 50 calendars per episode, 75-day base window, five
fixed outage days, and independent 20% daily weather loss.

## Results

| Remedy | Scenario full completion | Mean completion | Unscheduled P50 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Baseline | 13.2% | 85.6% | 127.5 |
| +1 shared crew | 13.2% | 85.6% | 127.5 |
| +1 shared crew + machine | 92.9% | 99.8% | 0.0 |
| 10% scope reduction | 23.4% | 90.2% | 72.0 |
| 90-day window | 41.5% | 93.2% | 34.0 |

## Interpretation

- Additional crew capacity alone has no effect because mowing and mechanical work also requires a
  machine. A shared crew plus machine is the strongest completion remedy.
- A 10% proportional scope reduction improves completion modestly but is not a substitute for capacity.
- A 90-day window helps substantially but remains below the capacity remedy.
- The capacity remedy's cost is not modelled. The comparison is about scheduling completion, not
  budget or procurement.

## Limits

- Added capacity and scope reduction are hypothetical tests, not contractor commitments.
- Scope reduction applies proportionally to work-item hours; it is not a risk-ranked deferral policy.
- Weather and outage assumptions are prototype values, not forecasts or asset-owner calendars.
- Only the first-year shift schedule is tested.
